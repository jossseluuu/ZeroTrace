Critical

Chrome: XML processing flaw already being exploited

Who it affects
Google Chrome on Windows, Mac, Linux, and Android before version 157.0.8103.50. Other Chromium-based browsers (Edge, Brave, Opera, etc.) usually get the same fix a few days later.
What to do
Update Chrome to 157.0.8103.50/.51 (Windows and Mac) or 157.0.8103.50 (Linux and Android): ⋮ menu > Help > About Google Chrome, then click Relaunch. On the Extended Stable channel, the fixed version is 156.0.8078.96.
Details
Google says an exploit for CVE-2026-125350 exists in the wild. It's a high-severity use-after-free bug in the XML component, reported on November 6 and fixed on November 9. CISA added it to its KEV catalog on November 10.

Critical

Outlook: a crafted file hands over your password hash, already exploited

Who it affects
Desktop Outlook in Microsoft 365 Apps and Office LTSC 2021 and 2024, without the November 10 security update.
What to do
Update Office: in Outlook, go to File > Office Account > Update Options > Update Now, then restart the app. Don't open unexpected Office files or attachments, even if they seem to come from someone you know.
Details
It's a spoofing vulnerability (CVSS 6.5) that Microsoft fixed in its November 10 Patch Tuesday, along with more than 1,000 other flaws. Opening a crafted file makes Outlook connect to an attacker's server and hand over the victim's NTLMv2 hash, which can be used to crack the password or impersonate the user on the network. It was exploited as a zero-day, and CISA added it to its KEV catalog on November 10.

Monday, November 9, 2026

Heads-up

OpenSSH on Alpine Linux: a very long password lets attackers log in without the real one

Who it affects
Servers and VMs running Alpine Linux (or another musl-based system without PAM) with the SSH server on, password login enabled, and some account without a password of its own. Containers that don't run sshd aren't affected, and neither are most distributions (Debian, Ubuntu, and Fedora use glibc).
What to do
Update openssh (apk upgrade -U -a) and restart sshd: upgrading the package doesn't restart the service. If you can't right now, set PasswordAuthentication no in your sshd configuration.
Details
With a password between 257 and 1,024 bytes, an attacker who can reach the SSH port can log in to accounts whose password field is * (key-only accounts, or root in the base image) if another account on the machine has a password. Alpine published it on November 5 alongside OpenSSH 10.6p2; vendors don't report active exploitation, but it's trivial to exploit.

Sunday, November 8, 2026

Heads-up

Apple: a 2024 WebKit flaw now under attack on unpatched devices

Who it affects
iPhones, iPads, Macs, and Apple TVs still running versions earlier than iOS/iPadOS 17.5, macOS Sonoma 14.5, or tvOS 17.5. If your device is up to date, you're not affected.
What to do
Update to the latest available version (Settings > General > Software Update). If your iPhone or iPad can no longer install iOS 17, avoid using it to browse unfamiliar websites and consider replacing it.
Details
It's a flaw in Apple's web engine (WebKit) that lets an attacker bypass pointer authentication, a memory protection; it's chained with other bugs to run code when you visit malicious web content. Apple fixed it in May 2024, but CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 4: there are real attacks against people who didn't update.

Critical

SonicWall: SSL VPN flaw that bypasses SAML, now exploited

Who it affects
Organizations with SonicWall Gen 7 or Gen 8 firewalls using SSL VPN with SAML sign-in (often with MFA enforced at the identity provider).
What to do
Update to SonicOS 7.3.4-7013 or later (Gen 7) or SonicOS 8.2.3-8015 (Gen 8). No workaround has been published. Gen 6 and older no longer get patches.
Details
Affects SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and earlier and 8.2.2-8015 and earlier. A user with valid credentials for another authentication method can bypass SAML checks, including MFA that depends on them. CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on November 6. If you run this VPN, review recent logins too.

Heads-up

Zyxel GS1900 switches: exploited flaw that slipped past the earlier patch

Who it affects
Small businesses and offices with Zyxel GS1900 series managed switches (GS1900-8, -8HP, -10HP, -16, -24, -24E, -24EP, -24HPv2, -48, and -48HPv2).
What to do
Install firmware V2.90 Patch 3 (for example, 2.90(AAHH.3)C0 on the GS1900-8) and don't leave the switch's web interface reachable by everyone on the local network.
Details
An out-of-bounds write in the web interface lets an attacker on the local network, with no password, run commands on the switch. Zyxel published its advisory on November 3. According to security firm Binarly, this is the flaw actually used in attacks on roughly a thousand switches previously blamed on CVE-2026-7273, and it works even with the June patch installed.

Who it affects
People using Firefox on desktop computers. This is not an alert for Chrome, Safari, or other browsers.

Critical

WordPress: update-process flaw now under attack

Who it affects
Anyone running a WordPress site, self-hosted or on a hosting plan. If someone else or an agency manages it, ask them.
What to do
Update WordPress to 7.1.4 or the latest security release for your branch, and confirm under Dashboard > Updates that it's applied. If your host auto-updates, verify it.
Details
On October 28, WordPress shipped a security release that fixes, among other issues, a weakness in the upgrade process that can allow code execution on the server during the upgrade window, and backported the fix to older supported branches. CISA added it to its KEV catalog on November 3 based on evidence of active exploitation, and security firms such as Wordfence have logged thousands of attack attempts.

Critical

Ivanti Connect Secure: corporate VPN hit by chained flaws

Who it affects
Organizations using this VPN. If you work remotely through it, give your IT team a heads-up.
What to do
Update Connect Secure to 22.8R2.6, 25.1.1.3, or 25.1.3.1 (or later, depending on the branch) and check for compromise: patching isn't enough if attackers already got in.
Details
On October 30, CISA added two of the flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (KEV); the third, an authentication bypass, had been listed since October 13. Chained together, they were used as zero-days. Volexity warns that Ivanti's Integrity Checker Tool doesn't catch this activity, so a clean result doesn't rule out compromise. Federal agencies face deadlines of November 2 and November 13.

Critical

PHP2Shell: two critical PHP flaws added to the exploited list

Who it affects
Anyone running a website or server on PHP: WordPress, online stores, forums, hosting control panels.
What to do
Update PHP to 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, or 8.2.35 (or later) and restart every worker. On shared hosting, ask your provider whether it's done.
